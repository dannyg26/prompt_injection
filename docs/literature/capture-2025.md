- Authors / venue / year / version read: Gauri Kholkar, Ratinder Ahuja (Pure Storage). Proceedings of the First Workshop on LLM Security (LLMSEC), pp. 176–188, August 1, 2025, Association for Computational Linguistics. Publisher (ACL Anthology) version (p. 1).

## What it does
- Introduces CAPTURE, a benchmark generator for "context-aware" direct prompt injections, built on the Framework / Separator / Disruptor (F/S/D) structure of Liu et al. (2023) (p. 2, §2). Two modes: MALICIOUS-GEN (context-aware attacks) and SAFE-GEN (benign prompts containing trigger words, to measure over-defense) (p. 2, §2).
- Grounds F in six application domains (Shopping, Covid, Movies, Stock, Travel, Python Code), using questions from public QA datasets expanded with GPT-4o (p. 2, §2.1).
- Evaluates five released guardrail classifiers (ProtectAIv2, InjecGuard, PromptGuard, Deepset, Fmops) and two LLMs as detectors (GPT-4o, Llama3.2-1B-Instruct) by FNR on MALICIOUS-GEN and FPR on SAFE-GEN, per domain (p. 3, §3; p. 4, Tables 2–3).
- Trains "CaptureGuard": three separate DeBERTaV3-base models (Python, Movies, Stocks), each trained on that domain's generated attacks and benign prompts **plus** the 14 benign and 12 malicious open-source datasets used by InjecGuard (p. 3, §2.4).
- Reports CaptureGuard accuracy on external benchmarks (NotInject, WildGuard, BIPIA-Injection), with baseline numbers copied from the InjecGuard paper (p. 4, §3; p. 12, Table 6).

## Data
- **Domain context (F) (p. 2, §2.1):** Quote: "For each domain, we started with a base set of questions (split into 30 train, 15 test, 15 validation examples), which were then significantly expanded using GPT-4o to generate 100 diverse examples per domain per split (see Table 4)." F expansion used gpt-4o at temperature 0.7 (p. 7, Table 4).
  - INFERENCE: the split of F is made on the 60 *seed* questions before GPT-4o expansion, so F seeds are disjoint across train/test/validation. Whether GPT-4o expansions from different seed sets are near-duplicates of each other was not checked. No near-duplicate or deduplication check was found after reading §1–§5 and Appendix A.1–A.4.
- **Attacks (MALICIOUS-GEN) (pp. 2–3, §2.2):** GPT-4o decomposes attacks from existing datasets (Safe-Guard prompt injection dataset [Erdogan et al. 2024], HackAPrompt [Schulhoff et al. 2023], Yugen.ai mixed-techniques dataset) into S and D (p. 2, §2.2; prompt at p. 9, Figure 3). D is augmented with "additional injection strategies from Table 7" (the HackAPrompt taxonomy of 32 attack techniques, p. 13, Table 7). Separators are rewritten by GPT-4o into "more evasive versions (S′) … to bypass common trigger-word detection" (p. 3, §2.2; prompt p. 10, Figure 4). Final prompts embed S′ and D in F.
  - Size: "This yielded 1274 training and 641 test/validation context-aware attacks" (p. 3, §2.2). Test and validation are reported as one combined count.
- **Benign (SAFE-GEN) (p. 3, §2.3):** S "specifically incorporates trigger words known to cause over-defense, drawn from NotInject"; D is a safe, relevant instruction; both generated by GPT-4o and embedded in F. "This process yielded 339 training and 171 test/validation benign samples across six domains" (p. 3, §2.3). Prompt: p. 8, Figure 2.
- **How train/test are separated:** only the F seed questions are stated to be split (p. 2, §2.1). Whether the source attacks, the decomposed D components, the S′ rewrites, or the NotInject trigger words are partitioned between train and test was **not found after reading §2.1–§2.4, §3 and Appendix A.1–A.4**. INFERENCE: the same source attacks, attack strategies (Table 7) and trigger-word vocabulary can appear on both sides, and both sides are produced by the same GPT-4o prompts at the same temperatures (p. 7, Table 4), so the test set is same-generator, same-template data relative to CaptureGuard's training set. The paper does not describe it as template- or family-disjoint.
- **Label quality:** Quote: "GPT-4o, used for data generation, is included as a strong baseline; potential evaluation bias is acknowledged, though human validation showed approximately 90% agreement with its malicious/benign classifications." (p. 4, §3). Number of annotators, number of items validated, sampling method and any agreement statistic (e.g. kappa) were not found after reading §1–§6 and Appendix A.
- **Count inconsistency (INFERENCE, unresolved):** the per-domain rates in Tables 2–3 imply denominators larger than the stated split totals divided by six. E.g. CaptureGuard Stock FNR 0.15% (p. 4, Table 2) is only reachable with ≥642 attacks in that domain's test set, and InjecGuard FPR 0.88% / 99.12% and Travel 99.71% (Tables 2–3) fit roughly 340–344 benign per domain, whereas the paper reports 641 attacks and 171 benign test/validation items across all six domains (p. 3, §2.2–§2.3). We could not reconcile this from the text (possibly per-domain counts, or test+validation pooled); per-domain test sizes are not stated anywhere read.
- Code: "The dataset generation pipeline code will be shared upon publication." (p. 1, footnote 1). Release not verified: UNVERIFIED.

## Detectors / models evaluated
- **Released classifiers (off the shelf):** ProtectAIv2 ("Fine-tuned deberta-v3-base for prompt injection detection"), InjecGuard (Li and Liu 2024, arXiv 2410.22770), PromptGuard (Meta 2024b), Deepset, Fmops (p. 3, §3; references p. 5–6). Operating thresholds for released detectors are not stated (not found after reading §3 and Appendix A); INFERENCE: native/default thresholds.
- **LLMs as detectors:** GPT-4o (temperature 0) and Llama3.2-1B-Instruct with a "Eliezer Yudkowsky"-style YES/NO prompt from Liu et al. (2023) (p. 3, §3; p. 7, Table 4; p. 11, Figure 5). GPT-4o is also the data generator (p. 4, §3; p. 5, §5).
- **Excluded:** LlamaGuard3 and WildGuard, "as our focus is not on jailbreaks and content moderation" (p. 3, footnote 2).
- **Trained here (CaptureGuard) (p. 3, §2.4; p. 12, Table 5):** DeBERTaV3-base; batch 32; LR 2×10⁻⁵; **max sequence length 64**; Adam; **1 epoch**; **classification threshold 0.5**. Quote: "We largely adopted hyperparameters and code from InjecGuard (Li and Liu, 2024)" (p. 3, §2.4). Quote: "Each domain-specific model was trained using (1) domain-specific sentences from MALICIOUS-GEN (Section 2.2) and SAFE-GEN (Section 2.3), and (2) the 14 open-source benign and 12 malicious datasets used by InjecGuard." (p. 3, §2.4).
  - How the domain data and the generic InjecGuard data are combined (mixing ratio, up-weighting, sampling) is not stated. Not found after reading §2.4, §3, Appendix A.3.
  - Only 3 of 6 domains get a CaptureGuard model; the choice is justified as covering preference-based, fact-based and technical domains (p. 4, footnote 3).
- **Ablations / comparisons relevant to C7 — none found.** After reading §2.4, §3, §4 and Appendix A.1–A.4, the paper does **not** report: (a) a generic-only retrained DeBERTa with the same recipe (the only generic comparator is the released InjecGuard model, whose training recipe is not reproduced here); (b) threshold adjustment or recalibration of any released or trained detector as an alternative to retraining; (c) equal-label-budget comparisons; (d) benign-only vs attack-labeled domain additions; (e) varying the number of domain examples. The threshold is fixed at 0.5 (p. 12, Table 5).
  - INFERENCE: CaptureGuard's in-domain gain is confounded with (i) adding labelled in-domain *attacks* as well as benign prompts, (ii) evaluation on held-out items from the same GPT-4o generator and templates, and (iii) the different training recipe from released InjecGuard. The paper's result shows that same-generator in-domain labelled data plus retraining lowers FNR/FPR on that generator's test set; it does not isolate benign-label adaptation or compare it with thresholding.

No AUROC, no fixed-FPR operating point (e.g. recall at 1% FPR), no threshold sweep. Not found after reading §3 and Appendix A.
- **Seeds / repeats / intervals:** no seeds, no repeated runs, no confidence intervals, no significance tests reported. Not found after reading §1–§5 and Appendix A.1–A.4. All numbers are single point estimates.

(p. 4, §3). So the Table 6 baseline numbers (including ProtectAIv2 BIPIA 8.67%) are InjecGuard's, not re-measured here. ## Findings relevant to our claims
- **C7 (matched benign labels vs threshold-only vs generic retraining at equal budget).** The paper retrains a DeBERTaV3-base on generic InjecGuard data plus in-domain generated attacks **and** benign prompts and reports near-zero FNR/FPR on same-generator in-domain test sets (p. 3, §2.4; p. 4, Table 2). It does not compare against threshold adjustment, generic-only retraining with the same recipe, equal label budgets, or benign-only additions (not found after reading §2.4, §3, Appendix A.3; threshold fixed at 0.5, p. 12, Table 5). Its test data share generator, prompts and (INFERENCE) attack/separator sources with training (pp. 2–3, §2.1–§2.3). OVERLAP = **none** for the controlled equal-budget comparison; the paper is an adjacent result (in-domain labelled retraining helps on same-generator data). C7's "UNVERIFIED vs CAPTURE" can be resolved: CAPTURE does not pre-empt C7.
- **C6 (ProtectAI v2 misses indirect/data-embedded injections, catches direct attacks).** The authors state their own data are direct, single-turn only and exclude indirect attacks (p. 5, §5). On their context-aware direct attacks ProtectAIv2 FNR is 14.98–30.60% (recall ≈ 69–85%) with FPR 27.06–82.27% (p. 4, Tables 2–3), threshold unstated. On BIPIA (Injection) ProtectAIv2 accuracy is 8.67% (p. 12, Table 6), a number copied from the InjecGuard paper (p. 4), at an unstated threshold. OVERLAP = **partial** (secondhand, consistent in direction with WBL p. 20 and PromptShield p. 9; no direct-vs-indirect contrast on a shared benign pool, no fixed-FPR operating point). Adds a third, secondhand "consistent with" citation; primary source is InjecGuard (not read; UNVERIFIED).
- **C3 (design effects; example-level intervals too narrow).** No intervals, seeds or repeats of any kind (not found after reading §1–§5, Appendix A). OVERLAP = **none**. Adds to the "no intervals" row of prior work.
- **C2 / C4 / C10 (group vs row splits; allocation).** Only the F seed questions are split (30/15/15) before GPT-4o expansion (p. 2, §2.1); no template/family-disjoint split of attacks, no row-vs-group comparison, allocation method for the seed split not stated (random or otherwise: not found). OVERLAP = **none**. INFERENCE: CAPTURE's in-domain test sets are an instance of same-generator/same-template evaluation of the kind C2 warns about.
- **C1 (template concentration of attack sources).** Uses HackAPrompt as an attack source and its 32-technique taxonomy (p. 2, §2.2; p. 13, Table 7), with no analysis of within-source template concentration. OVERLAP = **none**.
- **C9 (InjecGuard training file vs evaluation files).** Trains on "the 14 open-source benign and 12 malicious datasets used by InjecGuard" (p. 3, §2.4) and evaluates on NotInject/WildGuard/BIPIA (p. 12, Table 6); no overlap audit reported (not found after reading §2.4, §3, Appendix A). OVERLAP = **none**. INFERENCE: CaptureGuard inherits whatever train/eval overlap the InjecGuard data have.
- **C5, C8:** not touched. OVERLAP = **none**.

## Author-stated limitations (quoted, page-cited)
- "This study's focus on direct, single-turn prompt injections inherently limits its scope, excluding significant vectors like indirect and multi-turn attacks." (p. 5, §5)
- "Furthermore, attack diversity is constrained by the source datasets used." (p. 5, §5)
- "A primary limitation and area for future work is the reliance on a single powerful model, GPT-4o, for both data generation and as an evaluation baseline. This introduces a potential bias, as the generated data may inadvertently reflect the stylistic and logical patterns of the generator model, potentially giving GPT-4o an advantage in detection." (p. 5, §5)
- "GPT-4o, used for data generation, is included as a strong baseline; potential evaluation bias is acknowledged, though human validation showed approximately 90% agreement with its malicious/benign classifications." (p. 4, §3)
- On the InjecGuard comparison: "indicating a marginal trade-off in benign prompt detection" (p. 4, §3) — CaptureGuard NotInject accuracy 79.04% vs InjecGuard 87.32%.
- Not stated by the authors (our INFERENCE, not their limitation): absence of intervals/seeds; same-generator train/test; no threshold or budget ablation; max sequence length 64 may truncate longer context-embedded inputs; secondhand baselines in Table 6.
