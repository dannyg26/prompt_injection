## What it does
- Defines "over-defense" as "the tendency to predict malicious labels when benign sentences contain certain trigger words commonly used in prompt injection attacks" (p. 3, §3.1).
- Introduces **NotInject**, a benign-only evaluation set of 339 LLM-generated sentences. It has three subsets of 113 sentences each, containing 1, 2 or 3 trigger words (p. 1, Abstract; pp. 4–5, §3.2).
- Evaluates 5 released prompt-guard models and 3 LLMs on benign, malicious and over-defense accuracy (p. 5, §3.3; p. 7, Table 1).
- Trains **InjecGuard**, a DeBERTaV3-base classifier, on a merged set of open-source datasets plus 435 GPT-4o-mini-generated long-tail-format injections (p. 6, §4.1; p. 7, §5.1).
- Proposes **MOF** ("Mitigating Over-defense for Free"), which has three steps (p. 6, §4.2):
  - a "token-wised recheck", which feeds every vocabulary token to the trained model and flags tokens predicted as attack;
  - GPT-4o-mini generation of benign sentences containing the flagged tokens;
  - retraining from scratch on the augmented data.
- Reports ablations (p. 8, Tables 2–3) and an MOF sample-scale ablation (p. 14, §B.2; p. 16, Table 9).

### NotInject construction (pp. 3–5, §3.2; pp. 15–17)
1. **Trigger word identification.**
   - Word frequency lists are built from a malicious corpus D_m and a benign corpus D_b. Sources include "Alpaca … and Safeguard-Injection" (p. 4).
   - Words are ranked by frequency in each corpus. The score is the rank difference "∆r(w) = Rb(w) − Rm(w)", and the top k words are kept (p. 4; p. 15, Alg. 1).
   - The paper does not state k. Not found on pp. 4, 15.
   - The top 20 by rank difference are shown in Fig. 9: "PW, immoral, unfiltered, roleplay, uncensored, morals, Ignore, MUST, adversarial, sarcasm, freed, BREAK, subspecies, rib, ONLY, unrestricted, Remember, pretend, ignore, reckless" (p. 17, Fig. 9).
2. **Refinement.**
   - GPT-4o-mini (2024-07-18) filters the candidates (p. 4).
   - Then "three human evaluators with security expertise" independently rate each word on a 5-point frequency scale (Fig. 6, p. 16), and "words with an average score above 3 are identified as trigger words" (p. 4).
   - No inter-annotator agreement statistic is reported. Not found on pp. 4, 16.
   - Result: "the generated 113 trigger words" (p. 4).
3. **Corpus generation.**
   - GPT-4o-mini is prompted to write benign sentences containing a given number of trigger words (prompt in Fig. 11, p. 17).
   - "three subsets separately containing 1, 2, and 3 trigger words are built with 113 benign sentences per subset" (p. 4).
   - A GPT refinement pass follows (prompt in Fig. 12, p. 17), then "a manual review to confirm the safety of all sentences". The per-subset error ratio is plotted in Fig. 10 on a 0–10% axis (p. 5; p. 17). The values are not readable in the extraction.
   - Total: "339 generated samples with 113 for one-word subset, 113 for two-word subset, and 113 for three-word subset" (p. 5).

## Evaluation and statistics
- **Metric:** accuracy, "the proportion of correct predictions over the total number of test cases" (p. 6, §5.1). GFLOPs and inference time are also reported, with "Efficiency = Average Accuracy/Inference Time" (p. 7, Table 1 caption).
- **Aggregation:** INFERENCE, verified arithmetically against Table 7, p. 16. Each column in Table 1 is an unweighted mean of per-dataset accuracies, not a pooled accuracy:
  - Over-defense is the mean of the three NotInject subsets.
  - Benign is the mean of WildGuard and PINT benign.
  - Malicious is the mean of PINT injection and BIPIA.
  - Average is the mean of the three columns.
  - Check for ProtectAIv2: (76.11 + 47.79 + 46.02)/3 = 56.64 and (75.18 + 97.22)/2 = 86.20.

## Findings relevant to our claims
- **C1 (template concentration in the InjecGuard release).**
  - The paper is the source of the release. It gives per-source counts: hackaprompt-dataset 5,000 injection rows (p. 14, Table 5); TaskTracker, BIPIA_train, Question Set and others (p. 14, Tables 4–5).
  - It describes no template, level or group structure for any source, including HackAPrompt, and no deduplication (all pages read).
  - It does not explain the 6 groups among the 5,000 HackAPrompt rows (that would need the HackAPrompt paper).
  - **OVERLAP = none** (pp. 6, 14).
- **C2 (leave-one-template-out recall).** No template-held-out or source-held-out evaluation. The evaluation benchmarks are external datasets (p. 6, §5.1), but no split of training data is evaluated. **OVERLAP = none** (pp. 6–8).
- **C3 (design effects; example-level intervals too narrow).** No intervals, seeds or variance of any kind. Accuracies are unweighted means of per-dataset accuracies (p. 7, Table 1; p. 16, Table 7). **OVERLAP = none** (all pages).
- **C4 (row vs group split inflation).** Not addressed. **OVERLAP = none** (all pages).
- **C6 (ProtectAI v2 weak on indirect/BIPIA vs strong on direct).**
  - Table 7 reports ProtectAIv2 **8.67%** accuracy on BIPIA injections against **88.53%** on PINT injections. These are single-run accuracies at the model's native decision rule, with the BIPIA subset and size unstated (p. 16, Table 7).
  - LakeraGuard shows the same pattern: 12.00 on BIPIA vs 96.38 on PINT (p. 16).
  - The authors do not comment on the BIPIA gap; their narrative is about over-defense (pp. 5, 7).
  - **OVERLAP = partial.** This is a third prior source for the qualitative finding already marked ALREADY SHOWN. Its magnitude (8.67%) is close to WBL's 8.0%. It uses a different metric (accuracy, no FPR control), so it is not directly comparable to our recall at 1% FPR.
- **C7 (matched benign retraining ≈ threshold-only).**
  - Adjacent only. MOF adds 1,000 (text) or 762 (Table 4) generated benign samples and retrains from scratch.
  - This raises over-defense (benign trigger-word) accuracy from 64.31 to 87.32, and malicious accuracy moves from 75.95 to 77.39 (p. 8, Table 2).
  - The scale ablation shows a benign-vs-malicious trade-off: malicious 80.55 → 77.39 → 71.12 as samples rise from 500 to 2,000 (p. 16, Table 9).
  - There is no threshold-only comparison, no budget-matched design and no target-distribution adaptation.
  - **OVERLAP = none** (adjacent: generic benign augmentation fixes benign FPR on a benchmark whose generation procedure MOF shares; INFERENCE from p. 6 and p. 15).
- **C8 (1/(B+1) expected FPR).** No thresholds are set from labels. **OVERLAP = none**.
- **C9 (train file clean vs eval; validation = eval items; "paper discloses this at a high level").**
  - The paper confirms the release composition: the table sums equal 76,735 (p. 14, Tables 4–5).
  - It reports no train/eval overlap check (all pages).
  - **It never mentions a validation set** (zero hits for "valid" on all 17 pages; §5.1 training details on p. 7 omit checkpoint selection).
  - The disclosure clause is therefore **not supported by arXiv v3**. The ACL 2025 PIGuard version and the GitHub README are UNVERIFIED.
  - Separately, the paper openly reports choosing the MOF scale by the headline evaluation average (p. 14, §B.2; p. 16, Table 9). That is an author-stated selection on evaluation data, but it is not the validation-set issue.
  - **OVERLAP = none** (the paper does not audit its own contamination).
- **C10 (deterministic allocation).** No splits are described. **OVERLAP = none**.

## Author-stated limitations (quoted, page-cited)
- "the NotInject dataset, while carefully designed, may not fully capture the diversity of real-world benign inputs, particularly in domain-specific applications. This could result in the underestimation of models' over-defense tendency in complex, sensitive fields such as healthcare or finance." (p. 9, Limitations)
- The authors state no limitation on the training data composition, deduplication, validation or checkpoint selection, single-run results, or the absence of intervals. Not found after reading p. 9 (Limitations, Ethics) and all other pages.
- On MOF misidentification: "incorrectly identified words do not have a detrimental effect on the model" (p. 15, §C.2). This is an argument, not a measured result. No experiment is reported in §C.2.
