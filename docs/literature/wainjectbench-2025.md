## What it does
- It builds a benchmark for detecting prompt injection against web agents, with a text part and an image part. The text part has 991 malicious and 2,707 benign segments; the image part has 2,022 malicious and 948 benign images (p. 2 Fig. 1; p. 4 Tables 2–3).
- It categorises six attacks (VWA-Adv, EIA, Pop-up, WASP, WebInject, VPI) by attacker, capability, knowledge and contaminated observation (p. 3 Table 1, §3.2).
- It evaluates 6 text-based detectors (KAD, PromptArmor, Embedding-T, PromptGuard, DataSentinel, Ensemble-T) and 6 image-based detectors (GPT-4o-Prompt, LLaVA-1.5-7B-Prompt, JailGuard, Embedding-I, LLaVA-1.5-7B-FT, Ensemble-I), plus majority-vote ensemble variants (p. 5 Table 4; p. 6 §5.3).
- It reports TPR per attack and FPR per benign category (pp. 6–7, Tables 5–7).
- It reports AUROC and TPR at fixed FPR for the threshold-based detectors (p. 8; p. 10 Table 9).
- It runs a "domain adaptation" / cross-attack generalization experiment (p. 8, Fig. 2, Table 8), a caption-style control (p. 6; p. 10 Table 10), and one manual adaptive rewrite of WASP against PromptArmor (p. 8).

## Data
- **Malicious text.** Segments come from the attacks' outputs, grouped into four categories (p. 4 §4.1, Table 2):
  - Comment & Issue: WASP, 84.
  - Email & Msg: VPI, 31.
  - Image caption: VWA-adv, 298, of which 94 have explicit instructions (EI).
  - Web text: EIA 248 (62 with EI), Pop-up 216, VPI 114.
- **EI labelling.** "we manually examined all samples and labeled a text segment as containing explicit instructions only when both human annotators agreed" (p. 4 §4.1). No agreement statistic was found (pp. 1–12).
- **Benign text** (p. 4 §4.1, Table 2):
  - 806 comments/issues from Reddit (VWA) and GitLab (WA).
  - 173 ham emails/SMS from the Kaggle Spam Email and SMS Spam Collection datasets.
  - 226 image captions generated by LLaVA-1.5-7B.
  - 1,502 web-interface texts from VWA and Mind2Web.
  - Authors' rationale: "we deliberately constructed benign text segments that mirror the malicious ones." And: "This symmetric construction makes the metrics comparable across categories: a low FPR indicates that the detector captures attack-relevant patterns rather than distributional differences." (p. 4 §4.1)
- **Images** (pp. 4–5 §4.2):
  - EIA: "each segment with explicit instructions is inserted into eight webpage locations, producing 496 screenshots" (p. 4).
  - Benign: "we rendered 361 clean webpages ... We also captured their corresponding SoM-annotated screenshots" (p. 5), giving 722 benign screenshots.
  - The 298 VWA-Adv perturbed images are also used, through their captions, as the malicious caption text (p. 4 §4.2).
  - INFERENCE: this produces built-in near-duplicate clusters: 8 screenshots per EIA segment, and a plain/SoM pair per benign webpage.
- **Segment clustering.** "our Comment & Issue dataset contains 120 webpages and 806 segments, corresponding to an average of 6.717 segments per webpage" (p. 9 §7).
- **Main evaluation.** No train/test split. The whole dataset is the test set, and trainable detectors are trained on out-of-domain data:
  - Embedding-T: "we train the classifier on 500 benign and 500 malicious samples collected by DataSentinel" (p. 6 §5.4).
  - Image detectors: "1,000 benign samples from the COCO 2017 validation set [14] and 1,000 malicious samples from JailGuard" (p. 6 §5.4).
- **Domain-adaptation split.** "For each attack, we split its malicious samples (text or image) 50/50 into training and testing sets, and do the same for benign samples." (p. 8)
  - Not found after reading §4–§7 and App. A–D: whether this split is random, grouped by webpage or source segment, or seeded.
- **Deduplication / near-duplicate / template handling.** Not found after reading §4–§7 and App. A–D (pp. 4–12).
  - INFERENCE: a 50/50 row split of the EIA screenshots (8 per segment), or of the plain/SoM benign screenshot pairs, can place copies of the same source item on both sides. The paper does not address this.

## Detectors / models evaluated
- **Released, not retrained here:**
  - PromptGuard = "Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-86M [19] released by Meta" (p. 6 §5.4).
  - DataSentinel: "the publicly released large detection LLM from the authors" (p. 6 §5.4).
  - KAD with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1; PromptArmor, GPT-4o-Prompt and LLaVA-1.5-7B-Prompt with fixed prompts (p. 6 §5.4; p. 10 Fig. 3).
  - JailGuard with MiniGPT-4 and threshold 0.025 (p. 6 §5.4).
- **Trained here:**
  - Embedding-T: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 plus logistic regression (p. 6 §5.4).
  - Embedding-I: CLIP ViT-B/32 plus logistic regression, "with a threshold of 0.93" (p. 6 §5.4).
  - LLaVA-1.5-7B-FT: LoRA with rank 16, α 32, dropout 0.05 (p. 6 §5.4).
- ProtectAI and PIGuard/InjecGuard are not evaluated. Not found after reading §5–§6, Tables 4–10 (pp. 5–10).

## Evaluation and statistics
- **Metrics.** "We adopt two standard metrics ... True Positive Rate (TPR) and False Positive Rate (FPR)" (p. 6 §6). They are computed per attack column (Tables 5–6) and per benign category (Table 7).
- **Operating points.** Main results use each detector's native decision rule. Stated thresholds exist only for JailGuard (0.025) and Embedding-I (0.93) (p. 6 §5.4).
- **Fixed-FPR metrics.** AUROC and TPR at fixed FPR are reported for Embedding-T, Embedding-I, JailGuard and LLaVA-1.5-7B-FT (p. 10 Table 9). The text says: "including AUROC and TPR at fixed FPR levels of 0.5%, 1%, and 5%" (p. 8).
  - Table 9's column headers instead read "@FPR=0.5% @FPR=0.1% @FPR=0.01%" (p. 10).
  - Under those headers TPR increases as the FPR constraint tightens (e.g. Embedding-T 0.0071, 0.0272, 0.1191).
  - INFERENCE: the headers are mislabelled, and the text's 0.5/1/5% is the intended reading. Not found: how the thresholds were set (on the evaluation data or elsewhere).
  - The authors' reading of Table 9: "These results suggest that the poor detection performance is not simply due to the choice of operating threshold." (p. 8)
- **Confidence intervals.** One interval appears in the paper: "For WASP, which contains only 84 malicious comment segments, the corresponding TPR estimates have relatively wide uncertainty; for example, a TPR of 0.6071 has a 95% confidence interval of approximately [0.50, 0.71]." (p. 6)
  - The method is not stated. INFERENCE: it matches an example-level Wald binomial interval (0.607 ± 1.96·√(0.607·0.393/84) ≈ ±0.105).
  - No other intervals were found after reading §6–§7 and Tables 5–10 (pp. 6–10).
- **Clustering.** Clustering is acknowledged only for webpage-level FPR, under an independence assumption: "Assuming independent false positives across segments, the webpage-level FPR of Ensemble-T can be approximated as 1 − (1 − 0.0248)^6.717 = 0.155" (p. 9 §7). No group or cluster-level variance was found (pp. 1–12).
- **Seeds / repeats.** Not found after reading §5.4, §6 and App. A (pp. 6–10). Each result is a single number.

## Domain adaptation (§6, p. 8), the decisive question for Study 3
- **Purpose.** "A natural question is whether adapting these detectors to the web agent domain improves performance. To investigate this, we train detectors on data from a single attack and evaluate them across all attacks." (p. 8)
- **Data.** "Training uses the malicious training set of the chosen attack together with the benign training samples; evaluation is then performed on the testing malicious samples of all attacks plus the testing benign samples." (p. 8)
  - The added training data is **malicious plus benign**, both in-domain (50% of each attack's malicious rows, and 50% of the benign rows).
  - Sizes are implied only by the 50/50 rule applied to Tables 2–3. INFERENCE: 1,353–1,354 benign text rows, if all benign text rows are halved.
  - Not found: whether the benign training half covers all four categories or only the matching category.
- **Detectors.** Only Embedding-T and Embedding-I have reported results (p. 8 Fig. 2, Table 8). The text says "train detectors", but no adapted PromptGuard, DataSentinel or LLaVA-FT results were found (pp. 8–10).
- **Metrics.**
  - TPR: a train-attack × test-attack matrix (Fig. 2).
  - FPR on benign test halves, per category (Table 8).
  - No threshold change is described; Embedding-I keeps its 0.93 threshold per §5.4 (INFERENCE).
- **Results.**
  - "domain-adapted detectors often improve detection for the training attack but generalize poorly to others. For example, Embedding-T trained on WASP achieves a TPR of 0.90 on WASP ... but still yields a TPR of 0 on all other attacks." (p. 8)
  - Embedding-T FPR is 0.0000 in every cell after adaptation (p. 8 Table 8a). It was already 0.0000 before adaptation (p. 7 Table 7a).
  - Embedding-I FPR rises for some training attacks. Screenshot FPR is 0.2022 when trained on VWA-adv-S, 0.1357 for Pop-up and 0.1080 for EIA, against 0.0014 unadapted (p. 8 Table 8b; p. 7 Table 7b).
  - The authors: "this comes at the cost of elevated FPRs (e.g., 0.20 on benign screenshots when trained on VWA-adv-S)" (p. 8).
- **Comparison arms.** The only comparator is the unadapted, out-of-domain-trained detector (p. 8, "substantially better than the 0.00 TPR in Table 7a"; the TPR numbers are actually in Table 5, p. 7).
  - Not found after reading §5.4, §6, §7 and App. A–D: a benign-only addition, a curated/generic benign augmentation arm, a threshold-only arm, an equal-budget control, or any intervention aimed at reducing FPR.

**Verdict for Study 3: DOES NOT pre-empt.** Reasons, each page-cited:
1. The adaptation adds in-domain malicious and benign rows together. It never adds benign-only matched rows (p. 8).
2. Its aim is attack coverage (TPR), not over-defense. The one detector with text adaptation results (Embedding-T) had baseline FPR 0.0000, so there was no over-defense to reduce (p. 7 Table 7a; p. 8 Table 8a).
3. The only FPR change reported is an increase, for Embedding-I (p. 8 Table 8b).
4. There is no curated-versus-matched contrast, no threshold-adjustment arm, no equal-budget control, no seeds and no intervals (pp. 6–10).
5. The benchmark is web-agent text and images, not PIDS-Bench's externally sourced security-adjacent benign prompts. The detectors are an embedding plus logistic-regression classifier, not a fine-tuned DeBERTa (p. 6 §5.4).
- INFERENCE: the only link to Study 3 is the authors' premise that benign data "mirror[ing]" the malicious categories makes FPR a fair measure (p. 4). That premise is about evaluation design, not about benign augmentation as a fix.

## Findings relevant to our claims
- **C1** (template concentration in the InjecGuard release): not addressed. The paper describes built-in replication (EIA 8 locations per segment; plain/SoM screenshot pairs; p. 4, p. 5) but does no concentration analysis. **OVERLAP = none.**
- **C2** (held-out-group recall drop): the cross-attack experiment trains on one attack and tests on the others. In-attack TPR is 0.81–1.00 on the diagonal, and 0.00 off-diagonal for Embedding-T (p. 8 Fig. 2a). That is a held-out-source effect at attack/dataset level: same direction, different unit (attack source, not template/level), no fixed-FPR operating point, no interval. **OVERLAP = partial.**
- **C3** (design effects > 1; example-level intervals too narrow): one example-level interval (p. 6). Segment clustering within webpages is acknowledged only for webpage-level FPR, assuming independence (p. 9 §7). No design effect is computed. **OVERLAP = none** (INFERENCE: this may be cited as a further example of example-level intervals on clustered data).
- **C4** (row vs group split inflation, null): no row-vs-group comparison. The 50/50 split method is unstated (p. 8). **OVERLAP = none.**
- **C5** (withdrawn): **OVERLAP = none.**
- **C6** (a released detector is weak on indirect injection): the released detector here is Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-86M, not ProtectAI v2. Its average TPR is 0.0928, with 0.0000 on WASP and VPI and 1.0000 only on EIA w/ EI (p. 7 Table 5). It is consistent qualitatively (a released direct-injection detector fails on indirect web-agent text), but uses a different detector, a native threshold and no direct-attack contrast. **OVERLAP = partial** (a qualitative corroboration only).
- **C7** (matched retraining vs threshold-only vs generic, equal budget): in-domain retraining (malicious + benign) is compared only against the generic out-of-domain baseline, on the training attack's own test half. There is no threshold-only arm, no equal-budget generic arm and no benign-only matched arm (p. 8). **OVERLAP = none** (adjacent). This resolves the "UNVERIFIED vs WAInjectBench App. B" note in CLAIMS.md C7 for v2: App. B in v2 is an ethics statement (p. 10), and the §6 adaptation (p. 8) does not pre-empt C7.
- **C8** (threshold from B benign labels): not addressed. Table 9 reports TPR at fixed FPR but does not say how thresholds were set (p. 8, p. 10). **OVERLAP = none.**
- **C9** (InjecGuard validation-set contamination): not addressed. **OVERLAP = none.**
- **C10** (deterministic group allocation): the split allocation is unstated (p. 8). **OVERLAP = none.**

## Author-stated limitations (quoted, page-cited)
- "Our benchmark evaluates step-level detection of individual text segments and images, without modeling full multi-step agent-environment interaction dynamics." (p. 9 §7)
- "Moreover, we report sample-level TPR and FPR rather than webpage-level metrics. A webpage may contain multiple segments, so the webpage-level FPR can be higher than the reported sample-level FPR" (p. 9 §7)
- "although the benign emails and SMS messages used in our evaluation are drawn from real-world public datasets, they may not reflect realistic web agent observations." (p. 9 §7)
- "Finally, we do not evaluate adaptive attacks specifically optimized against the detectors." (p. 9 §7)
- "For WASP, which contains only 84 malicious comment segments, the corresponding TPR estimates have relatively wide uncertainty" (p. 6)
- Also stated (p. 9 §7): no evaluation of latency or computational overhead.
- INFERENCE (not author-stated): no seeds or repeats; an unstated split procedure for the adaptation experiment; no handling of the 8-location EIA replication or the plain/SoM screenshot pairing in splits; Table 9 header/text inconsistency (p. 8 vs p. 10); the text cites "Table 7a" for TPR values that are in Table 5 (p. 8).
