# Closest related work and novelty decision

Completed before data-repair implementation on 2026-09-23. See [reference verification](VERIFIED_REFERENCES.md) for versions, metadata discrepancies, and access failures. This is a bounded literature audit, not an exhaustive systematic review.

The table describes what metrics studies use, not their numerical performance. **Author-stated** denotes an explicit discussion/limitation read in full text. **Scope inference** denotes our comparison of the methods and experiments, not a claim the authors made. Neither proves global novelty.

| Paper | What it does | What it measures | What remains open / evidence status | How our narrowed question differs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [PIDS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15017v1) | Tests detectors under benign stress and distribution shifts; studies curated benign augmentation and calibration. | Detection metrics, hard-benign FPR, threshold trade-offs and seed variation. | **Author-stated, abstract and §7.6:** distribution-matched external augmentation is untested. | Compare annotation-budget-matched adaptation with threshold-only controls and a third frozen domain. |
| [CAPTURE](https://aclanthology.org/2025.llmsec-1.13.pdf) | Generates contextual data and trains domain-specific CaptureGuard models alongside generic data (§2.4). | FNR/FPR by domain and external-benchmark performance. | **Author-stated, §5:** single-turn/direct scope and generator dependence; **scope inference:** no matched human-label budget study in the experiments read. | Use independently sampled, human-adjudicated records and explicit data-budget controls, rather than claim domain-specific training is new. |
| [PromptShield](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15145v2) | Curates application/conversation data; varies model/data size and training composition (§5.4–5.5). | TPR at low FPR, AUC and benign-subset FPR. | **Author-stated, §7:** evolving distributions are outside its static training setup. | Separate target-label use for threshold selection from target-label use for weight adaptation at small equal budgets. |
| [InjecGuard](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22770v3) | Reduces token-trigger bias with generated benign examples and introduces NotInject. | Benign, malicious and over-defense accuracy, plus inference cost. | **Scope inference, §4–5 and Appendix B:** augmentation scale is studied, but not our human-label allocation comparison across target and untouched domains. | Isolate provenance of additional labeled records from their number. |
| [WAInjectBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01354v1) | Benchmarks web-agent text/image detectors; Appendix B explicitly evaluates domain adaptation. | TPR/FPR by content and attack category and transfer between categories. | **Observed finding, Appendix B:** adaptation can specialize to the training distribution and fail to transfer; it does not establish our proposed budget-efficient benefit. | Measure whether benign-domain adaptation preserves held-out detection and third-domain behavior under equal budgets. |
| [DataSentinel](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11358v4) | Trains a detector using a minimax objective and evaluates multiple NLP tasks. | FNR/FPR and downstream security outcomes. | **Scope inference, §V–VI:** experiments concern robustness of a trained detector, not a low-budget human annotation allocation study. | Study limited benign-domain labels and calibration without an adaptive attack-generation component. |
| [BIPIA](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14197v4) | Builds indirect-injection tasks and studies task-model defenses. | Attack success and ordinary-task output quality. | **Scope distinction:** these outcomes do not settle input-detector calibration or annotation efficiency. | Evaluate the detector as a component, not equate its recall with task-model protection. |
| [Jatmo](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17673v2) | Uses task-specific fine-tuning and teacher-produced outputs. | Task quality, security outcomes and training data requirements. | **Author-stated, §6:** task specialization sacrifices generality. | Adapt a reusable detector and measure retained behavior on an untouched domain. |
| [Defenses Against Prompt Attacks Learn Surface Heuristics](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.502.pdf) | Diagnoses position, trigger-token and topic shortcuts in tuned defenses. | Refusal behavior and benign task performance under controlled changes. | **Author-stated, §7:** a focused empirical analysis, not an exhaustive defense study; **scope inference:** does not answer our budget allocation comparison. | Test a controlled intervention with explicit data budgets, retaining failure diagnostics. |
| [EvoShield](https://doi.org/10.3390/math14101719) | The verified title and publisher-indexed abstract concern selective test-time adaptation with LLM querying. | Accuracy/macro metrics, attack recall/FNR, benign rejection, API use and runtime. | Published sections 4.1-5.4 checked in follow-up; routing/replay sweeps and ablations do not report the requested three-way equal-label-budget comparison. | Our offline equal-label-budget comparison differs from online routing/replay adaptation; author inquiry pending, global novelty not established. |

## Decision

**The broad question “does domain adaptation improve prompt-injection detection?” is already studied.** CAPTURE and WAInjectBench directly overlap it; PromptShield also studies data composition and scale. We withdraw that broad novelty framing.

The narrower candidate is **how to allocate a fixed, small label budget between threshold adjustment and domain-matched retraining while controlling harm on a third domain**. We did not establish that this exact comparison is already answered in the nine readable works above, but we also did not establish novelty. The follow-up resolution found no exact matching comparison in EvoShield's published experiments. PIDS-Bench's statement concerns its own experiment, not the entire field.

The experiment specification therefore proposes a controlled replication/extension. It must not use “first,” “novel method,” or an unqualified “unanswered question.” A capstone can defend a reproducible negative finding or a carefully isolated comparison, but publication positioning requires resolving the overlap first.

If EvoShield or another study already makes the same controlled comparison, candidate alternatives are (a) independently reproduce it and quantify how cluster deduplication changes its conclusions, or (b) measure expert annotation disagreement caused by missing task/trust-boundary context and its effect on detector evaluation. These are **alternatives requiring their own literature checks**, not promised novel contributions.
